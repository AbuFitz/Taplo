## Facts and decisions to resolve before publication

The owner supplied mytaplo.com, app.mytaplo.com, taplo.co.uk and hello@mytaplo.com as intended destinations. This does not verify mailbox operation, domain connection or an incorporated entity. Currently the public frontend is mytaplo.vercel.app. Editor content and demonstration booking/enquiry records use browser storage. The public Supabase client is anonymous and does not persist an authentication session. A browser draft is not an account. Paid plan selection does not purchase a subscription. Automated delivery, payment, public publishing and wallet/pass issuance must not be represented as connected without separate verification.

Before legal release, record the actual operator and controller; territorial scope; business/consumer customer policy and age requirements; processors, locations and international transfer arrangements; retention schedules; security and incident procedures; cancellation/refund/VAT terms; rights-request and complaints contacts; optional storage and marketing purposes; content moderation process; and actual support obligations. A policy cannot replace a missing implementation.

## Terms: proposed structure and substantive drafting direction

1. **Who provides Taplo.** Identify the legal operator, trading name, physical address, registration and VAT information where applicable, and an operational email. Explain how notices and complaints are made. Fill this from verified details, not a template company.
2. **What is being supplied.** Taplo supplies page-building and management tools. A business using Taplo remains responsible for its offers and fulfilment. Identify any payment/platform role precisely once connected. Example businesses and illustrative records are not proof of customers, reviews, availability or sales.
3. **Accounts and authority.** An authorised adult representative supplies accurate registration details and protects access. Explain permitted team access, role restrictions, recovery, suspension and termination. Do not describe browser-only drafts as authenticated accounts.
4. **Plans and subscriptions.** Present price, tax treatment, billing interval, included limits, renewal, cancellation, effective date, any trial terms and provider charges before purchase. Obtain separate active consent to optional extras. Preserve a durable copy of the agreement and purchase confirmation. No paid activation occurs merely from choosing a card or opening a preview.
5. **Consumer rights and cancellation.** Determine whether the actual purchaser is a consumer or business. Have applicable cancellation periods, any immediate-performance request and refund rules reviewed for the actual service. Do not copy digital-download exceptions into SaaS by assumption, blanket-waive statutory rights, or claim every sale is exempt.
6. **Customer transactions.** The business provides accurate descriptions, prices, availability, booking/cancellation rules, contact details and legally required seller information. A request is not confirmed until the stated acceptance step. Payment success, deposit capture and refunds must reflect provider results, not local UI state.
7. **Content and intellectual property.** Users retain their content and grant only the licence needed to host, display, transform for delivery and operate their selected public page. They must have rights and permissions for logos, images, staff profiles and uploaded material. Define removal/reporting and any termination/export effects. Do not take a perpetual unrelated marketing licence by default.
8. **Acceptable use.** Prohibit unlawful content, impersonation, fraud, infringement, malicious code, credential harvesting, abusive messaging and attempts to bypass access controls. Provide a reporting channel and proportionate review/removal process, with urgent restrictions where necessary and appeals where appropriate.
9. **Privacy and messaging.** Link the privacy notice and processor terms. Each business must assess its controller responsibilities for customer/staff data, lawful marketing and recipient preferences. Delivery and consent records need real controls; terms alone do not provide them.
10. **Third-party services.** Identify dependencies and separate agreements for payment, maps, reviews, messaging and wallet services. Do not promise delivery, ratings import, identity verification, finance eligibility or wallet support before it works. State any additional fees and data destinations before connection.
11. **Availability and changes.** Describe maintenance, service changes and security updates accurately. Do not promise an uptime target or priority onboarding service without an operational commitment. Material subscription changes need suitable notice and the rights applicable to the actual contract.
12. **Suspension and ending.** Specify grounds, notice where practicable, treatment of paid periods, export/deletion and any necessary retention. Do not claim local browser data can be recovered from a server when it cannot.
13. **Responsibility and liability.** Obtain review of proportionate business/consumer provisions against the actual risks and insurance. Do not exclude liability that cannot lawfully be excluded, use an arbitrary liability cap or purport to remove statutory consumer rights. Document security, backup and data-restoration obligations before promising them.
14. **Disputes, governing law and changes to terms.** Set these using the actual operator jurisdiction and customer markets. Preserve mandatory consumer protections. Version the document, date changes, retain acceptance evidence and give the notice appropriate to the change.

## Privacy notice: publication framework

1. **Identity and contacts.** Name the controller, contact details, any required representative and DPO, if applicable. Explain the distinction between Taplo’s own operations and business pages whose customer data is controlled by their owner.
2. **Data collected.** Inventory account identifiers, business profile content, staff data, bookings, enquiries, messages, uploads, payment references, preferences, support correspondence, technical requests and security records. State sources and which fields are necessary. Avoid collecting sensitive health information through general appointment forms merely because a clinic category exists.
3. **Purpose and lawful basis.** Select and document the applicable basis for each actual purpose before processing. Typical decision areas are providing the contracted service, account/security operation, legal obligations, support and optional marketing. Do not label every purpose “consent” or silently swap bases later. Record legitimate-interest assessments where relied on; check current UK guidance.
4. **Local preview handling.** Explain that local drafts and simulated submissions are stored on the current browser origin, are not automatically cross-device, and can be lost when browser storage is cleared. Normal hosting, image and external-link requests remain network activity. Do not promise that all information always remains on-device.
5. **Recipients.** List confirmed provider categories and, where useful, named providers. Document exactly what each receives. Public page publication makes selected profile content publicly accessible; private staff fields must stay private. A payment provider’s role is not automatically the same as Taplo’s.
6. **International transfers.** Verify actual hosting/support regions, destinations, adequacy or other safeguards and how a copy can be obtained. Do not invent a UK-only hosting promise or a transfer mechanism.
7. **Retention.** Approve a purpose-based schedule for accounts, customer records, billing, support, security logs and backups. Explain deletion/closure effects and legal holds. “For as long as necessary” without meaningful criteria is not a complete schedule.
8. **Rights and choices.** Explain the rights applicable to the purposes/bases, how to exercise them and how identity is checked proportionately. Address objection clearly, withdrawal where consent is used, access, correction, erasure, restriction and portability as applicable. Provide an operational route and a complaints process, including the ICO where appropriate.
9. **Storage and optional tools.** Inventory local storage/cookies and third-party embeds. Separate essential functionality from optional analytics/advertising, record the choice and allow changes. Do not ship an “accept all” banner for tools that are not actually installed, or install optional tools before the required choice.
10. **Automated features.** Describe actual palette extraction, suggestions and any third-party AI processing accurately. Do not claim solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects where none exist. If such processing is later added, assess it before deployment.
11. **Children and changes.** Set the audience/eligibility policy from the actual product and markets, then implement it. Version the notice and explain material changes. The earlier references to young entrepreneurs are not an age-gating policy.

## Processor agreement and supporting policies

Before handling real customer records for business owners, prepare a data-processing agreement matching the actual roles and processing, including instructions, confidentiality, security, subprocessors and changes, rights assistance, incident support, return/deletion, transfers and audit information. Prepare a real subprocessor list, security summary, acceptable-use/reporting policy, storage notice, cancellation information and complaints route. Consider accessibility information describing verified support rather than claiming blanket conformity.
