# Creative system and content production

All copy, names and example prices below are provisional original creative material. Fictional examples are never evidence of real Taplo customers or measured business outcomes. Keep Taplo's actual plan names, prices and entitlements unchanged.

Read UK-DIRECTION.md for the updated business identities, plain UK copy and photography brief; it supersedes the earlier boutique naming tone below. User clarification: build the intended live frontend, with contextual demo truth while backend is a later project.

## Position and homepage story

Working promise: **Your business, ready for the next customer.**

Supporting line: **Show what you do, what it costs and how to book or ask—all from one page.**

Main action: **Make your Taplo**. Secondary action: **Explore designs**. Use the same action labels through the site. Do not promise instant publishing, delivery, payment, imports or one-minute setup until the relevant operation is real and measured.

The visual signature should come from the transition between a business and its useful page. A strong photographed business moment is paired with a sharp, legible piece of Taplo UI. The platform shell can stay neutral; customer identities supply variety. Avoid presenting every section as overlapping phones or every feature as an icon tile.

| Scene | Copy direction | Focal visual | Interaction / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Your business, ready for the next customer. | One excellent business page with visible identity, price and next action; contextual business photography nearby | Open one service or stock detail without leaving the story; no misleading simulated success |
| First product story | From “how much?” to the next step. | A readable service row becoming service detail and booking/request review | Same service name, price and duration throughout |
| Identity | Make it look like you. | Same owner content in three structurally distinct looks | Real renderer; demonstrate controlled changes, no content replacement |
| Range | Different businesses. The same care. | Detailer, barber and dealership shown with their useful widgets | Each opens a meaningful populated example |
| Setup | Start with what you already know. | Owner enters a service; page fills with their words | Current setup UI, no fictional scrape or fake importer |
| Sharing | One page. Wherever customers find you. | Page becomes a real link, share card and counter QR composition | Sharp exports, actual target, print-safe QR |
| Plans | Choose the plan that fits your business. | Clear existing plan comparison | Preserve exact commercial rules; one source of truth |
| Final action | Put your business on the page. | Calm brand close with one action | Same onboarding destination |

Navigation: product exploration, designs, pricing, login and one primary start action. Product menu should group understandable customer tasks and show a relevant focal example. Footer should resolve useful destinations and brand closure without repeating the entire page.

## Example data and assets

`fixtures/businesses.json` is a source content brief, not Taplo's runtime schema. It gives stable IDs, original names, prices in integer pence, durations, statuses, copy and image needs. Adapt it through the existing BusinessContent schema rather than importing it directly. No availability in this file is live.

Photo commissioning order:

1. Detailer flagship hero, craft detail and workspace; first hero is generated and included.
2. Barber craft, workspace and three coherent fictional staff portraits.
3. Dealer inventory: three distinct vehicles with internally consistent angles for each, plus premises.
4. Café food/menu/interior; tutor portrait/workspace; trades craft and equipment.

Each final asset needs source/generation record, dimensions, alt text, subject, crop/focal point, usage rights/status and a meaningful filename. Avoid remote hotlinks and random photo service URLs. Optimize delivery copies separately from archival masters. Do not upscale a small screenshot and call it high resolution.

Real listing attributes must match the photo; generated generic car photography cannot prove a specific make, model, condition or listing. Fictional stock should remain labelled fictional, and generated images must remain illustrative. Never generate fake before-and-after proof or reviews implying actual service outcomes.

## Campaign concepts and production storyboards

### Campaign A: The next question

Audience: independent service businesses. Hook: **Prices? Times? Who with? Put the answers on your page.**

15-second vertical cut, original UI at readable scale:

- 0–2s: “How much is a cut?” over a close-up of the actual service price row.
- 2–5s: row opens to included work and duration; remove decorative movement while reading.
- 5–9s: choose eligible staff and time through the real demo journey.
- 9–12s: page identity pulls back to reveal one coherent business page. Demo outcome stays labelled; no fake payment or message notification.
- 12–15s: **Your business, ready for the next customer. Make your Taplo.**

Create a separate 16:9 layout and 4:5 layout. Recompose text/UI, do not crop the vertical render. This is a proposed pace, not a claimed optimal attention formula.

### Campaign B: Stock worth opening

Audience: small vehicle dealers. Hook: **The car. The details. A clear way to ask.**

6-second loop: clean available stock card → meaningful specification detail → vehicle-specific viewing enquiry. Use a paused end state, not a fake “just sold” ticker. Test a 15-second version that contrasts a vague social caption with the structured Taplo listing; do not name a competitor in the creative.

### Campaign C: More than a finish

Audience: detailers. Hook: **Show the finish. Explain the service. Make the enquiry easy.**

15-second cut: original workshop shot → relevant package and scope → request form using the chosen package → business page and QR close. Do not show a manufactured before/after transformation. If photo quote intake is not supported, the creative uses ordinary enquiries and lists photo intake as a future feature separately.

### Campaign D: Same business, unmistakably yours

Audience: style-conscious owners. Hook: **Your page should feel like your business.**

10-second identity film: the exact same content passes through three distinct theme compositions, each held long enough to understand; finish with the owner making a single meaningful adjustment. This specifically tests the engine rather than assembling unrelated screenshots.

## Film deliverables

Master projects, source media manifest, 16:9 site film, 9:16 social cut, 4:5 feed cut, posters and short capability loops. Use genuine current UI. Label an illustrative animation separately if it depicts a planned feature. Verify fonts/media are loaded before deterministic renders; inspect opening, each transition, representative middle and final frames plus full playback. Check UI text on an actual phone-sized display. Produce captions when speech exists, a silent-readable version, static fallback and no autoplay audio.

The first generated photo is an asset, not a finished ad. No finished film, exported campaign or licensed audio is claimed in this pack yet.

## Research plan for “better than Linktree”

Translate the aspiration into owner tasks: reach first useful draft, change a look while preserving content, add a service, reorder, configure a request, inspect a customer action, and share. Measure completion, errors, help needed and time with representative owners, comparing equivalent tasks fairly where access permits. Separately rate identity, readability, hierarchy, image quality and coherence using a consistent rubric. Establish the benchmark before claiming superiority or uplift.
