# UK business direction: content and visual decisions

24 October 2026, Europe/London. Owner instruction: credible, down-to-earth UK businesses; examples are a small part of the whole product. This brief updates the earlier creative material. The proposed look labels Stillwater/After Hours/Forecourt describe design studies, not a requirement to give every business a boutique identity.

## Decisions this research supports

What customers need before acting; wording and price conventions; useful collection density; natural premises/people imagery; how different business types should drive composition. This is a small local reference set, not a representative UK market study or evidence of optimal conversion.

## Voice and identity

Use direct service and location wording: “Haircuts, skin fades and beard trims”, “Used cars in Hertfordshire”, “See the menu”, “Ask about this car”. Let an owner add personality through their own words, pictures and chosen visual look. Avoid writing every small business as an editorial lifestyle brand. Avoid exaggerated slang, forced friendliness and generic claims such as trusted, best, premium or expert without support.

Use en-GB spelling, GBP and miles for UK examples. Service durations are useful for appointments; don't attach appointment language to café dishes. Keep fixed/from/quote/free distinct. Keep fictional status visible in the example environment without turning every paragraph into a disclaimer. No fabricated busy counters, recent sales, reviews or appointments to make it look alive.

## Photography and illustration commissioning

| Scene | Required cues | Reject when |
|---|---|---|
| Barber | Normal UK high-street shop, practical mirrors/chairs and working tools; ordinary daylight; a believable client interaction | It becomes a whiskey lounge, staged fashion editorial, empty chair showroom or caricature of a community |
| Detailer | Modest unit or credible driveway work area; equipment and work shown accurately; everyday vehicle as well as paint detail | Supercar-only mood replaces scope; a generated reflection or transformation is presented as proof |
| Dealer | Independent premises/yard, UK-style right-hand-drive vehicle context where visible, consistent setting and same-car angles | Grand glass showroom, US dealership cues, mismatched wheels/trim/colour across one listing |
| Trades | Specific task, relevant materials, practical domestic setting and clean workmanship | Unrelated power tools, impossible hand/tool relationships, implied certification |
| Tutor | Ordinary learning workspace, legible materials only if deliberately authored, natural portrait | Luxury office, fake school identity or credential badge |
| Café | Specific food, real portions, ordinary table/counter and coherent interior | Decorative stock flat-lay replaces the actual menu; invented dietary assurance |

Natural, well-lit and thoughtfully composed can still feel distinctive. UK identity must not depend on flags, fake licence numbers, stereotyped people or staged poverty. Do not infer ethnicity, gender or class from the phrase down-to-earth. Use relevant work and environment.
