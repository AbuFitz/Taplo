## The answer to the landing-page question

The business mini-site is the business's home **everywhere**, including inside Taplo. Social links, NFC cards, QR codes, Google profiles, Explore and saved businesses all lead to the same business identity and current offers.

Direct visitors get a brand-first mini-site. People arriving through Explore or Saved get that same mini-site with a small contextual way back. They should never feel that a business becomes an unrelated generic marketplace listing when they enter through Taplo.

Explore helps people choose. The business page helps them act. Activity helps them manage what happens afterwards. The app is useful for returning customers; installation should not gate ordinary browsing, shopping or booking.

## What I recommend changing

1. **Refine the block-based mini-space.** Compact identity and typed widgets; products browse sideways; details, cart, booking and requests use coherent popups/sheets with safe progress and return behaviour. More catalogue content should not make the public page endlessly taller.
2. **Make Studio task-first.** “Add what you offer”, “Change your look”, “Prepare today's orders”. Show relevant tools from operating capabilities. A category suggests a start rather than imprisoning the business.
3. **Simplify design choices.** Choose a composition, apply a curated look, add branding. Optional advanced controls come later. Content is edited once, wherever it appears.
4. **Design the rules before AI.** Stock, time, staff, resources, payment, fulfilment and customer permissions must agree. AI can later assist with the same validated operations.
5. **Choose the first complete business journey.** My recommendation is a small maker/studio selling physical goods and scheduled workshops. It tests native commerce, bookings, fulfilment, a branded page and repeat use together. It is a proposed pilot, not a restriction on the eventual platform.

## What could make Taplo worth choosing

A business can start with one offer, add another type of work, and keep one identity and one clear daily workspace. A customer can discover a workshop, book it, buy an object for collection and return to both through the same relationship.

This is a useful proposition to test. Modern Wix, Square, Shopify/Shop, Linktree and Beacons already cover substantial overlapping territory. “All in one” and “AI” alone are weak differentiation. The advantage must show up as less work and fewer confusing handovers for a defined customer.

Promising ideas in this pack include an offer readiness check, a customer “next step”, safe previews of schedule changes, reserving products for appointment collection, contextual rewards, and a single exception queue. None is claimed to be unprecedented.
