# Taplo's block-first direction

Working edition 0.7 · 31 October 2026. This is the current product/experience directive. It supersedes any earlier recommendation or example that suggests a conventional full website, a long product grid, or replacing the established widget system with editorial website sections. The previous NORTH image and route-selection explainer are not visual references for Taplo.

## The experience

A business has a compact, customisable collection of purposeful blocks. Customers see its identity, scan what matters, move sideways through products/media, and tap into useful detail or an action. The page is the business's branded mini-space. It should feel recognisably Taplo through its block construction and interaction quality while allowing genuinely different identities.

The three layers are: **page of blocks → contextual popup → action and ongoing activity**. Product details, cart, service details, booking selection, enquiries, rewards and supporting information use a coherent popup/sheet system where appropriate. A longer task can occupy the full mobile sheet while retaining the business identity and return path. A dedicated checkout route is a resilience/direct-link option, not a requirement to abandon the sheet-based experience.

Horizontal product showcases are the default. Product cards reveal the useful summary; long descriptions and secondary facts move into the detail popup. More products extend the rail or a deliberately opened collection surface, rather than making the public landing page indefinitely taller. Swipe is supplemented by controls and keyboard access.

## Recognisable without identical

Taplo's distinction should be a repeatable composition and interaction system: a compact identity cap; block headings, content and action corners that align; deliberately sized shelves; clear touch response; a popup that visually belongs to the selected block; and a small, predictable business action area. Some pages may be soft and expressive; others restrained or sharp. A mandatory yellow palette, identical profile layout or giant footer badge would undermine business identity.

Use a consistent structure underneath custom surfaces: known spacing families, coherent inner/outer corners, legible hierarchy, bounded content density, predictable Close/Back, truthful price/action placement and accessible target sizes. Brand style can vary. Transaction meaning, navigation safety, accessibility and record integrity remain dependable.

## What the attached mobile concept demonstrates

The revised FixNow concept uses its existing dark/yellow identity and actual product assets. It condenses the identity; groups a few services inside one widget with access to the rest; places dash cams in a horizontal shelf; opens product details with quantity and native cart actions; includes same-business recommendations; and makes cart/edit/return part of one sheet system. Example delivery/stock/payment states are design proposals, not newly verified merchant promises.

This is one composition to assess, not the only look every business must use. The broader architecture still covers commerce, booking, classes, quotes, rewards, hardware and support through these same connected records.

## Studio's job

Studio should make this flexibility understandable. Page-level choices establish the visual family. Selecting a block exposes only its content, layout and optional style exceptions. Editing an offer updates its single operational record. Popup design inherits the business style and can be previewed in place; it is not a second unrelated theme-builder project.

## Delivery order

First unify the design vocabulary and scope rules; then refine one complete page → item → cart/service flow; then apply the same contracts to the remaining blocks and operational journeys. Preserve useful existing work, but replace arrangements and controls when evidence supports it. No current schema or screen is immune from improvement.
