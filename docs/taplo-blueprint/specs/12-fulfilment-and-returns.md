# Delivery, collection, digital access and returns

Status: workflow recommendation. Legal policy requires jurisdiction-specific review.

Each order line requires an appropriate fulfilment method. Start with a deliberately chosen scope: collection and simple domestic shipping are candidates for the maker pilot; local delivery and digital fulfilment can follow separate validated rules. Do not launch every combination just because the data model can name it.

Collection: received → preparing → ready → collected. Record collection location, hours/instructions and who may collect. A code or staff confirmation can record handover; a public order number alone is not sufficient proof. Shipping: preparing → dispatched → carrier updates → delivered/exception. Record partial fulfilment and quantities rather than applying one label to a mixed order.

Digital access is a separate entitlement with verified delivery, download limits if chosen and revocation rules after refund. Do not imply that all digital purchases have identical cancellation rights.

Return request: identify item/quantity/reason → apply applicable policy and rights → approve or explain → receive where required → inspect → refund/replacement → close. Distinguish merchant approval from completed payment reversal. Record shipping responsibility, damaged goods and returnless resolutions. An automated rejection must not override statutory rights.

Acceptance: one dispatched item and one awaiting collection display accurately. A refunded returned unit is not automatically resellable until stock disposition is recorded. A customer sees the current next step and can contact the merchant from that order. A carrier exception enters the owner's work queue. A refund's pending or failed status is visible instead of a false “done”.
