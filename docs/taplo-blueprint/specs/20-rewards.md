# Merchant rewards with accountable balances

Current continuation · 2.6: access, repository/domain, native release, rewards/Wallet/widget/scanner and launch decisions are consolidated in the new root documents linked from START-HERE. These resolve earlier open method/binary/domain questions; actual implementation/provider/legal evidence remains required.

Status: recommended first loyalty model. Evidence: Square's documented reward/refund behaviour in source record.

Begin with merchant-specific rewards. Owners choose a small set of understandable rules: eligible paid spend or completed visits, reward threshold, exclusions and expiry where permitted. Show the terms before customers rely on the reward. Platform-wide rewards are a separate economic and settlement proposal.

Use an append-only reward ledger: pending earn, confirmed earn, redemption reservation, redemption, expiry and reversal, each linked to the originating transaction/rule version. Balance displays derive from the ledger. Record money and points separately; points are not automatically cash, transferable credit or stored money.

Choose when rewards vest: after fulfilment/completion or another documented qualifying point. Cancelled/no-show work should not earn merely because an appointment was created. Partial refunds reverse the eligible contribution under the rule, not an arbitrary full points deduction. Refunding an order bought with rewards must restore or reverse value according to published terms, with duplication protection.

Present rewards in the business context and in the customer's private view. Saving a business and joining its rewards programme can be separate actions. Do not enrol a customer in marketing because they collect a stamp.

Acceptance: retrying a completion event grants one earn. Two concurrent purchases cannot redeem the same final reward. A partial return produces an auditable adjustment and clear customer explanation. Programme closure addresses outstanding promises rather than silently deleting balances. Abuse controls cover repeated tiny transactions, fake visits and staff self-awards.
