# Deterministic intelligence before AI

Status: recommended engine behaviour. Depends on: domain rules in 10–17.

“Smart” starts with predictable assistance: show a field only when it matters, select safe defaults, validate conflicts and explain the next action. The system can recommend a layout from available content without inventing business facts.

Proposed offer readiness check: combine presentation readiness, transaction eligibility and fulfilment readiness into specific actions. Examples: “Set where customers collect this”, “This service has no available staff”, “Your workshop can take eight people but this room holds six”. Avoid a decorative percentage that conceals why the offer cannot work.

Proposed change preview: before changing working hours, fulfilment rules or service duration, show affected future commitments. Changes to an offer apply prospectively. Existing agreed work requires an explicit reschedule/change process and customer communication.

Proposed exception queue: collect cross-system problems with a clear recovery owner — expired stock holds, payment awaiting reconciliation, unassigned appointments, failed notifications and unanswered requests. Separate urgent problems from optional growth suggestions.

If AI is added, it submits the same validated commands as manual UI. Preview consequential changes; do not let generated copy silently alter money, booking rules or customer commitments. Preserve audit history and allow undo where possible.

Acceptance: an owner understands why an offer is unavailable and can resolve it from the prompt. Conflicting settings cannot be saved merely because AI proposed them. Recommendations never claim credentials, legal policies, stock or times the business has not supplied.
