# 68 · Research, construction and pre-user validation

Edition 1.0 · Goal: eliminate avoidable assumptions before observing real users. No amount of desk research guarantees that users will reveal only minor issues.

## Evidence ladder

1. Current primary documentation: confirm capabilities/rules and provider limits; record exact source, retrieval date and what it establishes.
2. Actual task inspection: observe equivalent workflows where authorised, including setup, normal use and exception recovery. Record environment/plan/date; documentation alone is not a usability test.
3. Domain walkthrough: simulate concrete mixed business scenarios against the records, permissions and states. Label simulations as internal, never user evidence.
4. Designed interaction: populated mobile/desktop references and meaningful empty/error/keyboard variants.
5. Implementation/provider validation: real persistence, race conditions, permissions, device handovers and failure recovery.
6. Independent owner/customer observation: attempt tasks with minimal coaching; capture misunderstanding, assistance and completion.

## Construction before participant sessions

Choose complete reference scenarios across tutoring, mobile work, retail and workshops. Define inputs, source records, expected transitions, customer wording, owner task, support recovery and data exposure. Include novice owner with no logo/photos, mixed-capability merchant, staff-linked social entry, last-slot/stock conflict, private address, late cancellation, pending payment and merchant exit. A designer/developer unfamiliar with the conversation should be able to explain the outcome from the pack; log questions they have to invent answers for.

Perform a structured cognitive walkthrough: what is the user's goal; is the next action visible; does its wording predict the effect; can they tell what happened; can they recover? Review accessible reading/focus order and actual contrast; test keyboard and text enlargement. Review operational and customer copies of the same event side by side. Resolve inconsistent promises before customer sessions.

## Research gaps still requiring deliberate work

This edition checked focused primary sources. It is not a complete 2026 competitor field study, observed operator interviews, comprehensive legal/provider diligence or proof of the best pricing. Broader competitor research already retained in the pack remains contextual; reverify precise plan/pricing/availability claims before relying on them. Staff-linked page research establishes a familiar use case, not the commercial rights of chair renters. No invented persona quote or usability score counts as evidence.

## Observations and decisions

Capture task outcome, unprompted route, hesitation/misunderstanding, assistance, error/recovery, participants' own wording and relevant device. Avoid leading with “Would you like this feature?” Ask about recent real work and alternatives, then watch the task. Small early rounds can expose large issues; choose diverse enough owner/customer contexts to avoid confirming only the founder's habits. Recruitment/sample size is a practical research plan, not a guarantee of statistical coverage.

Classify findings by consequence: unsafe/wrong commitment or disclosure; blocked task; repeated confusion; recoverable friction; visual preference. Fix high-consequence failures before polishing stylistic preferences. Re-run affected connected paths after material changes; do not blanket-repeat every test on every minor wording edit. If users reveal a major model problem, change the model rather than declaring the research failed. A good blueprint reduces preventable rework while keeping room for evidence.
