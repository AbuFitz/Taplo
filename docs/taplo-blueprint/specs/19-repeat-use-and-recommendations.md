# Returning customers and useful next actions

Status: opportunity hypotheses.

Taplo's customer value should continue after discovery. Proposed repeat actions include rebook a previous service, reorder a still-available product, use an eligible package credit, view a receipt, find collection instructions and return to a saved business.

Rebook carries forward appropriate preferences, then revalidates current staff, duration, time, price and policy. Reorder creates a new cart from current purchasable variants; it does not promise old pricing or stock. State what changed before commitment. Never carry forward private treatment notes into an unrelated public form.

Proposed personal next-step display can combine due actions across businesses while keeping each relationship distinct. Examples: one quote to approve, tomorrow's appointment, one order ready to collect. Offer the most useful task first, with understandable ordering and no pressure tactics.

Suggestions within a merchant's page should stay within that merchant's catalogue unless the customer deliberately returns to Explore. A matching purchase, fitting add-on or workshop material can be useful when compatible. Explain bundle price, stock and scheduling consequences. “Smart” should mean relevant, not constant upselling.

Acceptance: rebooking an archived service offers a clear alternative without silently substituting a different commitment. A saved business that closes remains linked to accessible historic records. Customer controls can hide a reminder or leave a business relationship without deleting records required for an existing transaction. Experiment success measures task completion and repeat voluntary use, not just notification opens.
