## Separate relationships

Taplo ↔ merchant software contract; merchant ↔ customer supply contract; Taplo ↔ customer account/discovery service; Taplo ↔ buyer of Taplo hardware; merchant ↔ worker/independent operator; Taplo ↔ processor/registrar/hosting/messaging suppliers. Do not pretend one universal terms checkbox settles these relationships. Identify contracting entity and legal seller at the relevant point. An employee's professional page is not a separate seller merely because its name is prominent.

## Specific current-source implications

Government online-selling guidance identifies pre-order information and durable confirmations [R5]. CMA guidance warns against unfair cancellation/deposit terms [R24]. Taplo therefore needs policy evaluation and evidence, not merely a “non-refundable” toggle. Statutory rights cannot be removed by a merchant theme or checkbox; precise exceptions for dated leisure, services, personalised goods and digital supply require review of the actual offer and jurisdiction.

FCA guidance says marketplaces/booking businesses receiving customer money may provide regulated payment services and that exclusions have conditions [R22]. Using a processor does not alone settle perimeter responsibility. HMRC's current platform guidance includes tests based on connecting sellers/customers and access to payment amounts, with narrower software-only exclusions [R21]. Given Taplo's intended native commerce/discovery, assess scope explicitly rather than assume it is only a page builder. These are assessment requirements, not a conclusion that Taplo definitely needs authorisation or reports every seller.

Reviews are not harmless decoration: CMA guidance addresses publishers of consumer reviews [R25]. Ofcom's checker specifically considers user-to-user features and messaging/reviews [R31]; assess the actual proposed service, applicable exemptions and duties. Do not label Taplo an in-scope general search service merely because it has an internal business search box.

## Subscription date caution

The earlier consultation response anticipated spring 2027 [R27]. Following the October 2026 guidance link exposed a PDF explicitly labelled draft: it says the relevant draft regulations require parliamentary approval, describes the regime as not yet in force, and sets out a prospective 1 January 2027 application [R34]. This clarifies the status of the landing page [R28] and the draft instrument [R29]. A prospective date in draft guidance is not enacted commencement. Record the made legislation/commencement/transitional position with qualified review at release and on changes. Design modular reminders/cancellation/cooling-off handling now, without applying B2C rules indiscriminately to B2B SaaS.
