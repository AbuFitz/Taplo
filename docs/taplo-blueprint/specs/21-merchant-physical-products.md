# Physical goods linked to services

Status: native commerce capability. Distinct from Taplo's own hardware business.

Businesses may sell finished goods, limited batches, made-to-order goods and goods with an associated service. Each needs honest availability and a fulfilment promise. A product image block is presentation; the product/variant and inventory are operational records.

Proposed useful connection: reserve a product for appointment collection. Example: a customer books a pottery workshop and buys a mug to collect then. The order and booking stay distinct, with an explicit fulfilment link. If the workshop is cancelled, ask whether the customer wants ordinary collection, shipping if supported, or the applicable cancellation resolution; do not automatically cancel an independent product purchase.

Product plus fitting is a stronger operational coupling. Define compatibility, part stock, staff skill, installation time and what is refundable if one component becomes unavailable. Begin with narrowly validated combinations, not arbitrary bundles of any item and any service.

A mini-site can support a modest catalogue with collections and item routes. Large catalogues need searchable collection views, but no owner should have to design a traditional multi-page shop before selling a few items.

Acceptance: the appointment collection link appears in both owner's order and appointment views. A cancelled booking does not leave the product stranded without a next step. Sold-out goods cannot be added through an old block. Variant imagery, price and stock agree across the page, detail and checkout. A substitution requires customer agreement rather than a silent warehouse change.
