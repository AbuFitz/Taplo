# Platform identity and boundaries

Current continuation · 2.6: access, repository/domain, native release, rewards/Wallet/widget/scanner and launch decisions are consolidated in the new root documents linked from START-HERE. These resolve earlier open method/binary/domain questions; actual implementation/provider/legal evidence remains required.

Status: recommended architecture; revisable. Owner: product. Depends on: customer identity, offers and access control.

Taplo is the customer product. Studio is the business operating environment. Console is the internal environment. Explore is a customer destination, not a fourth standalone product requiring a separate account or duplicate business profile. Marketing explains the service; Inspiration helps owners choose a visual starting point.

A business owns one canonical identity with stable internal ID, editable public handle, brand, seller information, locations, service areas and publication settings. A person may be a customer and a member of several businesses. Personal and business contexts are explicit; switching workspaces must not mix their private information.

Use one offer model with distinct operational types: physical product, digital product, appointment, scheduled group session, request/quoted work, package and membership. Do not force every type into a price-plus-duration structure. A unified interface does not require a single giant database table.

A published business page reads the same identity and eligible offers used by discovery. Studio controls them; Console supports them through permitted actions. A customer's Activity reads their transactions, rather than reconstructing them from the current page.

Acceptance: renaming a business changes its current presentation while an old receipt retains its transaction-time seller details. Disabling Explore removes discovery eligibility without destroying direct access or private receipts. Removing a staff membership immediately removes access to that business. Console sees no more customer data than its role and case require.

Open: final naming and URL structure, custom-domain entitlement, multi-location scope, launch jurisdictions. Prefer a modular application initially; these boundaries describe responsibilities, not a requirement for microservices.
